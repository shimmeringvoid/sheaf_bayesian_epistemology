Next-token maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) gives language models fluency, yet likelihood under a pooled corpus is neither necessary nor sufficient for truth. Building on Lopez-Paz's analysis of invariance, we develop a three-layer Bayesian and cellular-sheaf epistemology. Layer 1 (Provenance): at its population optimum, pooled MLE weights latent sources by corpus prevalence, not reliability. Repetitions within one channel saturate at a finite Bayes factor, whereas known-independent channels compound exponentially. Under latent provenance, unverified corroboration is bounded under any common-origin prior floor and grows only polynomially under a Chinese Restaurant Process; a sound verifier of distinct derivations restores near-exponential growth. Layer 2 (Consilience): we separate nuisance framings from epistemic angles and pair off-policy GFlowNet Trajectory Balance with a verified-signature Product-of-Experts reward and Chow's abstention rule. Layer 3 (Knowledge Sheaves): we prove Weyl-Wedin stability of approximate harmonic subspaces and derive an exact scalar map for warm-started Student-$t$ sheaf filtering in the strong-coupling gross-fault limit, with threshold $S_{\mathrm{fault}} < S_{\mathrm{hon}}$ in exponent-weighted source counts. In simulation, a two-stage filter (weighted spatial-median centring, then Student-$t$ EM) resists capture by up to $\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor$ colluders near the origin, and farther out once the offset exceeds about 10-15 times the map discrepancy $\sigma_{\mathrm{map}}\|x_{*,v}\|_2$; unverified clone majorities are outvoted only while verified honest sources keep that inequality. A three-stage variant, given $\sigma_{\mathrm{map}}$, lowers the margin to about five and restores direct-reading accuracy without faults, but leaves faulty vertices uncorrected at moderate offsets. Spacetime sheaf selection and a two-step Hodge fault rule are also tested.
